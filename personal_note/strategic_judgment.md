Decision 1. Zero-Click Content Should Be the Default Strategy

Decision

Publish content that delivers complete value directly on LinkedIn. Use external links only when they genuinely improve the reader's understanding or provide additional resources, such as documentation, case studies, or implementation guides.

Why?

Amanda Natividad strongly advocates zero-click content to maximize native engagement and reader retention, while Richard van der Blom shows that external links are not inherently harmful when used appropriately. I believe the objective should not be to avoid links entirely, but to optimize the reader's experience. If an external link provides meaningful value beyond the post itself, it should be included intentionally rather than avoided by default.

Decision 2. Build Authority Before Optimizing Reach

Decision

Focus on creating content that resonates deeply with the ideal customer before trying to maximize impressions or reach.

Why?

Chris Walker, Peep Laja, Anthony Pierri, and Lashay Lewis consistently emphasize attracting the right audience rather than the largest audience. I believe authority creates more qualified opportunities than popularity. By speaking directly to the ideal customer, content naturally attracts better prospects, reduces unnecessary questions, and allows conversations to focus on real business needs instead of explaining basic fit.

Decision 3. Founder-Led Branding Is the Best Starting Point

Decision

For early-stage B2B SaaS companies, founder-led content should be the primary growth engine.

Why?

Dave Gerhardt and Guillaume Moubeche demonstrate that people trust people more than logos. A founder who actively shares knowledge, communicates transparently, and engages with customers can build trust much faster than a corporate brand. However, as the company becomes more established and gains brand recognition, company-led branding becomes increasingly valuable alongside founder-led communication.

Decision 4. Frameworks Improve Consistency, but Judgment Creates Differentiation

Decision

Use frameworks as editing and quality-control tools, not as creative limitations.

Why?

Richard van der Blom provides repeatable structures that improve consistency, while Dave Gerhardt emphasizes originality and authentic communication. I believe frameworks should provide direction, but judgment should shape the final output. Combining both creates content that remains structured while maintaining its own voice, personality, and unique perspective instead of simply copying existing formats.

Decision 5. Audience Problems Should Always Outweigh Trends

Decision

Every content idea should begin with a real customer problem. Trends should only be used when they naturally reinforce that problem.

Why?

Lashay Lewis focuses on bottom-of-funnel customer intent, while Todd Clouser demonstrates how trends can increase visibility. I believe problems are business opportunities in disguise, and solutions become the products or services that customers ultimately choose. Success depends on how effectively a solution addresses the customer's problem. Trends can amplify visibility, but they should never replace solving genuine customer needs.

Decision 6. Measure Business Outcomes, Not Vanity Metrics

Decision

Success should be measured by qualified conversations, pipeline growth, and business impact rather than likes, impressions, or follower counts.

Why?

Chris Walker consistently argues that demand creation should generate measurable business results. I believe engagement metrics only matter when they contribute to meaningful outcomes. A post can receive thousands of likes yet produce no qualified leads, while a smaller audience with higher relevance may generate significantly greater business value.

Decision 7. AI Should Accelerate Execution, Not Replace Thinking

Decision

Use AI to accelerate research, drafting, organization, and idea generation, while keeping human judgment responsible for strategy and decision-making.

Why?

This principle appears across multiple experts and is also reflected in how this repository was built. Personally, I see AI as a tool that improves efficiency, organizes information, generates suggestions, and acts as a collaborative assistant. However, strategy, creativity, prioritization, and final decisions should always remain human responsibilities. AI should enhance thinking, not replace it.

Decision 8. Learn From the Entire Ecosystem, Not From a Single Guru

Decision

Build strategies by comparing multiple expert perspectives instead of following one authority blindly.

Why?

Every expert in this research contributes valuable insights, but each also has limitations and biases. Studying multiple practitioners provides broader perspectives, more implementation options, and stronger critical thinking. I believe this approach leads to more balanced, adaptable, and practical strategies than relying entirely on a single methodology. This philosophy is also the foundation of this research repository.
