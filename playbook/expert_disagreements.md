# Expert Disagreements — LinkedIn Organic Content Strategy for B2B SaaS

This document includes only strategic tensions supported by the recommendation library. Recommendations that address different stages, conditions, or tactics that can be used together are excluded.

## Link strategy in LinkedIn posts

### Expert A — Amanda Natividad

- **Recommendation:** Create a LinkedIn asset that delivers a complete, useful idea without requiring a click.
- **Evidence:** Natividad defines a zero-click artifact as one that can stand alone; she suggests removing the link to test whether the idea remains valuable.
- **Citation:** Recommendation 15, [Amanda Natividad video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J98cUdZl-JQ), `youtube-transcripts/amanda_natividad_J98cUdZl-JQ.md`.

### Expert B — Richard van der Blom

- **Recommendation:** Test a cluster of three or more outbound links when sources are integral to the post, rather than applying a blanket no-link rule.
- **Evidence:** Van der Blom reports that posts with three or more outbound links perform 20% better than posts with one link.
- **Citation:** Recommendation 68, [Richard van der Blom post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/richardvanderblom_20-things-the-data-says-about-linkedin-in-activity-7474027987458260993-JHK1), `linkedin-posts/richard_van_der_blom_post_28.md`.

### Strategic Tension

The recommendations differ on whether the post itself should be designed to work independently of off-platform navigation or should deliberately include a substantial set of outbound sources. The first treats on-platform value without a click as the design constraint; the second treats multiple links as a potentially beneficial distribution element.

## Primary content-performance objective: resonance or awareness reach

### Expert A — Amanda Natividad

- **Recommendation:** Make the idea specific enough to resonate before trying to extend its reach, and do not broaden it until it becomes forgettable.
- **Evidence:** Natividad states “resonance BEFORE reach” and “Reach without resonance is just noise.”
- **Citation:** Recommendations 6–7, [Amanda Natividad post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/amandanat_jay-acunzo-has-talked-for-a-long-time-about-activity-7473133780807995392-hQW0), `linkedin-posts/amanda_natividad_post_9.md`.

### Expert B — Richard van der Blom

- **Recommendation:** Use polls for awareness reach, while recognizing that they are not evidence of audience conviction or high-quality engagement.
- **Evidence:** Van der Blom reports polls have the highest reach multiplier but the lowest engagement multiplier, describing them as formats that “travel everywhere and convince no one.”
- **Citation:** Recommendation 70, [Richard van der Blom post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/richardvanderblom_20-things-the-data-says-about-linkedin-in-activity-7474027987458260993-JHK1), `linkedin-posts/richard_van_der_blom_post_28.md`.

### Strategic Tension

The recommendations prioritize different primary outcomes for content selection. Natividad places meaningful audience response before broad distribution; van der Blom identifies a format to maximize broad awareness despite its weak engagement quality.

## Channel philosophy: founder-led organic growth or channel-agnostic selection

### Expert A — Guillaume Moubeche

- **Recommendation:** Build in public through founder-led content rather than relying on paid advertising as the growth mechanism.
- **Evidence:** The captured framework is described as scaling to $10M ARR with “No ads, just founder-led content,” beginning with “Build in public.”
- **Citation:** Recommendation 26, [Guillaume Moubeche post](https://linkedin.com/post/dummy1), `linkedin-posts/guillaume_moubeche_post_1.md`.

### Expert B — Dave Gerhardt

- **Recommendation:** Select the channel that fits the product, timing, team, budget, and strategy; do not assume any channel is inherently required or excluded.
- **Evidence:** Gerhardt says YouTube, LinkedIn, direct mail, webinars, TikTok, email, events, PR, partnerships, and paid analyst spend can all work; the challenge is choosing for the available ingredients.
- **Citation:** Recommendation 22, [Dave Gerhardt post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/davegerhardt_spoiler-alert-everything-in-marketing-works-activity-7472966743162945537-oz8X), `linkedin-posts/dave_gerhardt_post_34.md`.

### Strategic Tension

Moubeche presents a founder-led, no-ad growth model. Gerhardt rejects channel absolutism and frames channel selection as contingent on the company’s situation, including options outside founder-led organic content.

## Content-development philosophy: prescribed data-backed framework or independent creative judgment

### Expert A — Richard van der Blom

- **Recommendation:** Use the six elements—hook, promise, positioning, core message, conclusion, and call to engage—as an editing checklist for LinkedIn posts.
- **Evidence:** Van der Blom says high-performing posts share this structure and advises drafting naturally, then checking for the six elements.
- **Citation:** Recommendations 27–35, [Richard van der Blom post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/richardvanderblom_my-gift-to-you-6-ingredients-of-high-performing-activity-7473260256018534400-u7as), `linkedin-posts/richard_van_der_blom_post_29.md`.

### Expert B — Dave Gerhardt

- **Recommendation:** Use creative judgment and conviction instead of mechanically following content best practices.
- **Evidence:** Gerhardt tells marketers to “use your brain,” be creative, and not follow best practices.
- **Citation:** Recommendation 25, [Dave Gerhardt post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/davegerhardt_spoiler-alert-everything-in-marketing-works-activity-7472966743162945537-oz8X), `linkedin-posts/dave_gerhardt_post_34.md`.

### Strategic Tension

Van der Blom supplies a repeatable, performance-derived set of post components. Gerhardt cautions against treating established best practices as the basis for content decisions, emphasizing independent creative judgment instead.

## Topic selection: audience-specific problems or platform-level trending topics

### Expert A — Amanda Natividad

- **Recommendation:** Build a standalone asset around a problem the company is uniquely qualified to solve, and generate ideas from a specific audience decision.
- **Evidence:** Natividad says the asset should address a problem the company is uniquely qualified to solve and recommends using a hyper-specific decision prompt.
- **Citation:** Recommendations 19–20, [Amanda Natividad video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J98cUdZl-JQ), `youtube-transcripts/amanda_natividad_J98cUdZl-JQ.md`.

### Expert B — Richard van der Blom

- **Recommendation:** Test AI-related content because it is the platform’s top topic and creators are underusing it.
- **Evidence:** Van der Blom reports AI as the number-one topic on LinkedIn, with a 1.72x reach multiplier.
- **Citation:** Recommendation 67, [Richard van der Blom post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/richardvanderblom_20-things-the-data-says-about-linkedin-in-activity-7474027987458260993-JHK1), `linkedin-posts/richard_van_der_blom_post_28.md`.

### Strategic Tension

The first approach selects topics from the company’s distinctive ability to solve a defined audience problem. The second introduces platform-wide topic momentum as a reason to select or test a topic. These are different bases for deciding what to publish.
