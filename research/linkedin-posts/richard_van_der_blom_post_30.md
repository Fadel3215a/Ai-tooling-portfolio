        # Post Content

        LinkedIn just launched its Creator Marketplace. Great news, if you live in the US. The rest of us? We wait. Again!

This is a pattern of a platform that preaches equality and opportunities for all. The Video Tab. Creator monetisation features. New tools that roll out in America first, while creators in Europe, Asia, Latin America, and the Middle East get no timeline and no explanation.

I'm not against LinkedIn building a Creator Marketplace. Connecting brands with creators in a structured way makes sense, and they should have done this way earlier. What I'm against is the assumption that LinkedIn's creator community is American.

It isn't.

Some of the most consistent, high-quality content on this platform comes from outside the US. And yet, when new features drop, international creators are routinely last in line.

Last week I reached out to (and commented on) 6 different posts from LinkedIn employees sharing the news, asking if they could explain why these choices are being made like this. None of them responded (so far).

But that's not all. 

Our data suggests LinkedIn has been nudging creators toward official brand collaboration channels for a while now. Quietly but firm, through the algorithm.

→ Toggle the Brand Collaboration label? −45% reach.
→ Add a brand partnership hashtag? −35% reach.
→ Use both together? Up to −65% less performance.

That's done by design.

LinkedIn's algorithm treats signals of external brand partnerships as a reason to suppress distribution. You're doing a deal outside their ecosystem, so their ecosystem doesn't promote it.

Now that they're launching their own Creator Marketplace (with brands paying to find creators inside the platform) the commercial logic becomes clear.

𝗧𝗵𝗶𝘀 𝗺𝗮𝘁𝘁𝗲𝗿𝘀 𝘄𝗵𝗲𝘁𝗵𝗲𝗿 𝘆𝗼𝘂 𝗱𝗼 𝗯𝗿𝗮𝗻𝗱 𝗱𝗲𝗮𝗹𝘀 𝗼𝗿 𝗻𝗼𝘁. Any post that looks like a sponsored collaboration may be taking a reach penalty. So, check your language, check your hashtags, tags and CTA's.

LinkedIn building a monetisation layer for creators is a legitimate business move. But international creators deserve to be part of it from day one. And everyone deserves to know the algorithm may already be clearing the path at our expense.

If LinkedIn is serious about being the world's professional platform, it needs to act like it.

Are you inside or outside the US and have you noticed the difference?
