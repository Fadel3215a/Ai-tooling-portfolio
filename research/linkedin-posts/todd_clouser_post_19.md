        # Post Content

        The problem with a lot of AI is that it's supposed to make us more efficient, but we spend huge amounts of time trying to get it to produce what we're looking for, when we could've just created it manually faster to begin with.

Especially with images.

Thumbnails are something I've been creating manually since I started my first YouTube channel in 2011.

But it's also a piece of my content workflow that, if it worked well, I'd be totally guilt-free handing over to AI. 

But I’ve never been able to find anything that solved three things at once:

1. Maintaining a true likeness of the person in the video
2. Keeping brand guidelines incorporated
3. Actually saving time/repeatably generating good results, instead of just swapping editing for prompting and re-prompting time.


A few weeks ago I tried Luma AI and it nailed the first two.

Photorealism: I could take a screenshot of a podcast guest from a Zoom recording and turn it into an image that looked ready for a YouTube thumbnail.

Brand: Paste in a screenshot of brand guidelines and an example from a previous podcast thumbnail, and it would replicate them near perfectly.

But I was still swapping Photoshop or Canva for an AI editor. 

Now, it works as a board vs a traditional chat, so it’s easy to click/reference/pull from multiple images to teach it what I wanted, which works great, but I wasn’t really solving for the third problem (becoming more efficient). 

Then last week, Luma released the API for their Uni-1 model, which I started playing with over the weekend and it’s finally solving the time-saving problem, which has always been my biggest complaint with AI image generation. 

Now I can build thumbnail generation straight into a Claude skill, using the exact parameters I was already using to make them manually.

And because that Claude skill is already pulling timestamps from the transcript for other content, thumbnails get generated automatically the moment a new podcast gets recorded. 

To test it out, I set it up to see if it could repeatably create Colin and Samir-type thumbnails (in our branding with our guests) with nothing but a podcast transcript and screenshot. 

Turned out pretty spot on.
